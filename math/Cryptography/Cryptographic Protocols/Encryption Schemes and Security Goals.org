#+title: Encryption Schemes and Security Goals
#+setupfile: ../../../setup.org

Encryption begins with a communication problem: Alice sends a message to Bob
over a channel observed by Eve. Three different guarantees can be required:

- *Confidentiality:* Eve does not learn the message.
- *Integrity:* Bob receives the same message that Alice sent.
- *Authenticity:* Bob can establish that Alice sent the message.

The first three lectures focus on confidentiality. Integrity and authenticity
are separate goals and are not consequences of confidentiality alone
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons].

* Secret-key and public-key encryption

Let \(S_A\) and \(S_B\) denote secret information available to Alice and Bob,
and let \(\mathsf{PUB}\) denote public information.

- In *secret-key encryption* (SKE), Alice and Bob share secret information:
  \(S_A=S_B\). Encryption and decryption use the same secret key.
- In *public-key encryption* (PKE), Alice encrypts using public information,
  while Bob decrypts using secret information unavailable to Eve.

The lectures begin with SKE, also called a Shannon cipher.

* Ciphers and correctness

A cipher is a pair of algorithms \((E,D)\) over a key space \(\mathcal K\),
message space \(\mathcal M\), and ciphertext space \(\mathcal C\):

\[
E:\mathcal K\times\mathcal M\to\mathcal C,
\qquad
D:\mathcal K\times\mathcal C\to\mathcal M.
\]

It must satisfy *correctness*:

\[
\forall k\in\mathcal K\;\forall m\in\mathcal M,
\qquad D(k,E(k,m))=m.
\]

Correctness only says that honest decryption recovers the message. It says
nothing about what an adversary can infer from the ciphertext.

* Introductory examples

The shift cipher encrypts each letter by moving it a fixed number of positions.
It illustrates correctness, but its tiny key space and visible language
structure make it insecure.

For messages of length \(L\), the one-time pad (OTP) takes

\[
\mathcal K=\mathcal M=\mathcal C=\{0,1\}^L
\]

and defines

\[
E(k,m)=k\oplus m,
\qquad
D(k,c)=k\oplus c.
\]

Correctness follows from \(k\oplus k=0^L\). Its security and unavoidable key
length are developed in [[id:64853E70-8C7D-44AF-B1C4-DFE3B3FCFABC][Information Theoretic Security]]. Replacing its long random pad
with efficiently generated pseudorandomness leads to
[[id:22E49935-1278-487C-AB40-7F33726C5C6B][Pseudorandom Generators and Computational Encryption]].

The computational security goal used for practical ciphers is defined in
[[id:AE9054EE-2979-4C79-9B85-B247C6C12C1F][Computational Security and Indistinguishability]].
