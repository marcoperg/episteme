A collision bound quantifies the risk that independently sampled values
repeat. In cryptographic proofs, repetitions often mark the point where an
ideal independent-pad simulation ceases to match a random-function game.

* Collision probability

Let \(R_1,\ldots,R_Q\) be independent uniform elements of a set of size
\(N\). Let \(\mathsf{Bad}\) be the event that \(R_i=R_j\) for some
\(i<j\). Each pair collides with probability \(1/N\), so the union bound gives

\[
\Pr[\mathsf{Bad}]\leq
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{\binom Q2}{N}\right\}.
\]

The pair events need not be independent. For \(Q\leq N\), the exact
probability is

\[
\Pr[\mathsf{Bad}]=1-\prod_{j=0}^{Q-1}\left(1-\frac jN\right).
\]

For \(Q>N\), a collision is certain. The birthday scale is
\(Q\) on the order of \(\sqrt N\), not \(N\)
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons secs. 5.3 and 5.6.1].

* Comparing games until a bad event

Suppose two games are coupled on one probability space and their success
indicators agree whenever \(\mathsf{Bad}\) does not occur. Then

\[
\lvert\Pr[W_0]-\Pr[W_1]\rvert\leq\Pr[\mathsf{Bad}].
\]

Indeed, pointwise
\(|\mathbf1_{W_0}-\mathbf1_{W_1}|\leq\mathbf1_{\mathsf{Bad}}\).
Take expectations and use the triangle inequality. No independence between
success and the bad event is assumed.

* Example: random indices in encryption

PRF-based masking samples \(r_i\in\{0,1\}^d\) on each query. After
replacing the PRF by a random function, new indices give fresh pads, but a
repeated index gives the old pad. Its deviation from an independent-pad game
is at most \(\binom Q2/2^d\).

Adaptive plaintext selection does not invalidate this bound: the encryption
algorithm still chooses fresh independent indices. For an adversary making
at most \(Q\) queries, pre-sampling \(Q\) hidden indices also covers early
termination.

* Identify the right collision

Repeated *inputs* to a random function reuse an output. Coincident *outputs*
at distinct inputs are allowed and do not violate independence. A proof must
say which event actually breaks its simulation; it cannot merely attach a
birthday term to an unspecified repetition.
