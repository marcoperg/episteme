A hybrid argument connects two experiments by smaller changes that can each
be related to a known assumption. The main work is defining the intermediate
experiments and simulating their transitions efficiently.

* Probability calculation

Let \(H_0,\ldots,H_t\) be experiments or distributions with a common
adversarial interface. Write \(p_i=\Pr[A(H_i)=1]\). Then

\[
\lvert p_0-p_t\rvert
=\left|\sum_{i=1}^t(p_{i-1}-p_i)\right|
\leq\sum_{i=1}^t|p_{i-1}-p_i|.
\]

An endpoint gap therefore implies some adjacent gap of at least
\(|p_0-p_t|/t\). But existence of an index is not yet an algorithm for
choosing it uniformly across security parameters
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 3.2].

* Random-transition reduction

Suppose one challenge can simulate \(H_{i-1}\) in its real case and \(H_i\)
in its ideal case, for each \(i\), with the same output orientation.
An efficient reduction chooses uniform \(I\in\{1,\ldots,t\}\), embeds the
challenge at transition \(I\), and returns \(A\)'s output. Its advantage is

\[
\left|\frac1t\sum_{i=1}^t(p_{i-1}-p_i)\right|
=\frac{|p_0-p_t|}{t}.
\]

This is the average of *signed* gaps before taking an absolute value. It is
not the average of their absolute values. The exact telescoping identity
avoids guessing the signs of the individual gaps.

* Example: stretching a PRG

In sequential expansion, \(H_j\) makes the first \(j\) output bits and the
current state uniform, then continues the generator. A challenge inserted at
step \(j\) simulates the earlier or later hybrid according to whether that
challenge is generated or uniform. The reduction loses a factor equal to the
number of generator steps.

For GGM, the transition is a tree *level*, and only queried prefixes are
represented. An exponentially large conceptual tree does not require an
exponentially large simulator.

* Limits of the method

A polynomial number of transitions and polynomial simulation cost preserve
computational security. An exponential hybrid chain can erase the useful
advantage or require an inefficient reduction. Each application must also
justify its own correlations and adaptive queries: saying "by a hybrid
argument" does not replace that analysis.
