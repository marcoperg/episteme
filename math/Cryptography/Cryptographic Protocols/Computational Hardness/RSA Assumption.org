The RSA assumption asserts average-case inversion hardness for one specified
RSA key-generation family. It is not proved by the algebraic correctness of
RSA and must not be replaced by the weaker assertion that factoring is hard.

* Inversion experiment

Fix the prime-length function \(\ell(\lambda)\), public odd exponent \(e\),
and admissible-prime sampler of RSA key generation.

1. Generate \(N=pq\) and \(d\) using that sampler.
2. Sample uniform \(X\in\mathbb Z_N\) and compute \(Y=X^e\bmod N\).
3. Give \((N,e,Y)\), with public parameters, to \(A\).
4. Accept if it returns \(\widehat X\in\mathbb Z_N\) with
   \(\widehat X^e=Y\pmod N\).

Because RSA is a permutation, acceptance is equivalent to
\(\widehat X=X\). Define \(\operatorname{RSAAdv}[A]\) as this success
probability. The assumption says it is negligible for every PPT adversary
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.9].

This formulation samples from /all/ residues, not just \(\mathbb Z_N^*\).
Conditionally on \(N\), the image \(Y\) is also uniform on \(\mathbb Z_N\).
The probability includes prime generation, the sampled input, and adversarial
coins. Easy individual inputs such as \(0\) or \(1\) do not refute an
average-case hypothesis when their total probability is negligible.

* The direction of the factoring implication

An efficient factorer for the same modulus distribution gives an RSA inverter:
recover \(p,q\), compute \(\varphi(N)=(p-1)(q-1)\), compute
\(d=e^{-1}\bmod\varphi(N)\), and return \(Y^d\bmod N\).

Every successful factorization yields a successful inversion. Therefore

\[
\text{efficient factoring}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{efficient RSA inversion},
\]

or, contrapositively,

\[
\text{RSA inversion hardness}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{factoring hardness}.
\]

The reverse implication is not established: there could be an inverter that
does not reveal a factorization. RSA-based reductions here assume RSA
one-wayness itself, not merely factoring hardness
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.9.1].

* Scope and connections

The parameter-generation distribution matters. A claim for one exponent or
modulus family must not silently be transferred to another. Nor does inversion
hardness make deterministic raw RSA a semantically secure cipher.

- [[id:69560A39-4283-597D-9A11-69E69EFE6B78][Factoring]] has its own experiment and sampling conditions.
