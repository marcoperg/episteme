A PRF authenticates a message by making its tag look like an unpredictable
table entry. Tagging other messages must not reveal the value at a fresh one.

* Construction

Let \(F:\mathcal K\times\mathcal M\to\{0,1\}^t\) be a secure PRF.
Define

\[
\mathsf{Tag}(k,m)=F(k,m),\qquad
\mathsf{Ver}(k,m,\tau)=\mathbf1_{\{\tau=F(k,m)\}}.
\]

Reject malformed inputs. Equality verification gives correctness and unique
tags. An \(\ell\)-bit-input PRF authenticates exactly \(\ell\)-bit messages,
not arbitrary-length strings
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 6.4].

* Forgery bound

For every adversary \(A\) making at most \(Q\) tagging queries, there is a
PRF distinguisher \(B\) making at most \(Q+1\) oracle queries such that

\[
\operatorname{MACAdv}[A,\mathcal I]
=\operatorname{sMACAdv}[A,\mathcal I]
\leq\operatorname{PRFAdv}[B,F]+2^{-t}.
\]

Unique tags give the equality. Forgery success probabilities need no factor of
two.

* Reduction and the fresh-entry argument

Given oracle \(O\), answer tagging queries with \(O(m)\) and record their
messages. Reject a malformed or nonfresh candidate \((m^*,\tau^*)\); otherwise
query \(O(m^*)\) once and test equality with \(\tau^*\).

- With \(O=F_k\), acceptance is exactly the real forgery event.
- With \(O=R\), a random function, condition on the full view and the legal
  fresh candidate. Lazy sampling has not assigned \(R(m^*)\); it is still
  independent uniform in \(\{0,1\}^t\). The chance of guessing it is
  \(2^{-t}\).

Illegal or nonfresh candidates make random-game acceptance /at most/
\(2^{-t}\), not necessarily equal to it. PRF security bounds the change in
acceptance probability, proving the inequality.

* Tag length is a separate requirement

Blind guessing on a fresh message succeeds with probability exactly \(2^{-t}\),
even for a random function. One-bit tags therefore permit forgery with probability
\(1/2\).

For asymptotic security, require \(2^{-t(\lambda)}\) negligible, equivalently
\(t(\lambda)=\omega(\log\lambda)\). A fixed multiple
\(t=c\log_2\lambda\) gives the non-negligible probability \(\lambda^{-c}\)
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 6.4.1].
