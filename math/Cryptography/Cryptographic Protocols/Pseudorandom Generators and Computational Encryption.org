#+title: Pseudorandom Generators and Computational Encryption
#+setupfile: ../../../setup.org

A pseudorandom generator expands a short, uniformly random seed into a longer
string that no efficient algorithm can distinguish from uniform. This permits
an OTP-like cipher with a key shorter than the message, at the cost of replacing
perfect security with computational security
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons].

* Pseudorandom generators

For the usual bitstring spaces, a PRG is an efficiently computable function

\[
G:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^{\ell},
\qquad \ell>n,
\]

such that every PPT distinguisher \(\mathcal A\) has negligible advantage

\[
\operatorname{PRGAdv}[\mathcal A,G]
=\left|
\Pr_s[\mathcal A(G(s))=1]
-\Pr_r[\mathcal A(r)=1]
\right|,
\]

where \(s\) and \(r\) are uniform in \(\{0,1\}^n\) and
\(\{0,1\}^{\ell}\), respectively. Expansion implies that most
\(\ell\)-bit strings are not outputs of \(G\); pseudorandomness says that this
structural fact cannot be detected efficiently.

* The computational one-time pad

Define

\[
E(k,m)=G(k)\oplus m,
\qquad
D(k,c)=G(k)\oplus c.
\]

Correctness follows as for the OTP. To establish security, compare two games:

1. The *real game* masks \(m_b\) with \(G(k)\).
2. The *ideal game* replaces \(G(k)\) by a uniform \(r\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\).

The ideal game is an OTP game, so its output distribution is independent of
\(b\). If an adversary distinguishes either real game from its ideal version,
then a reduction can use its challenge string as the pad and distinguish PRG
output from uniform. Thus

\[
\operatorname{SSAdv}[\mathcal A,(E,D)]
\leq
\operatorname{PRGAdv}[\mathcal B_0,G]
+\operatorname{PRGAdv}[\mathcal B_1,G],
\]

which is negligible. This is a standard game-hopping pattern: replace a
computational object by an ideal random object, prove the ideal property, and
reduce any observable gap to the underlying assumption.

* Sources of pseudorandomness

The lectures sketch three concrete candidates without giving complete security
proofs:

- *Blum-Micali:* \(G^{(1)}(x)=(g^x\bmod p,\operatorname{MSB}(x))\).
- *Blum-Blum-Shub:* use \(f(x)=x^2\bmod N\), for
  \(N=pq\) with \(p\equiv q\equiv3\pmod4\), together with
  \(\operatorname{LSB}(x)\).
- *A group-based candidate:* \(G(a,b)=(g^a,g^b,g^{ab})\) under an appropriate
  Diffie-Hellman indistinguishability assumption.

The exact domains, encodings, permutation restrictions, and assumptions matter.
The sketches alone are not complete constructions. The general route through
one-way permutations is developed in
[[id:91B8E8A6-F67B-4918-AFB4-6AD0087AC6C8][One-way Functions and Hard-core Predicates]], and polynomial output length in
[[id:A0997AA0-C919-44A4-BF11-86385CB65969][PRG Stretch Amplification and Hybrid Arguments]].

* Stateful stream ciphers

To process blocks without fixing the stream length in advance, let

\[
G:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^{n+B}.
\]

On state \(\mathsf{sk}_i\), split

\[
G(\mathsf{sk}_i)=\mathsf{sk}_{i+1}\mathbin\Vert r_i,
\]

where \(r_i\in\{0,1\}^B\). Encrypt and decrypt a block by

\[
c_i=m_i\oplus r_i,
\qquad
m_i=c_i\oplus r_i.
\]

Both parties update to the same next state. The construction has no predefined
final block, but sender and receiver must remain synchronized. This operational
description is not yet a proof for literally unbounded interaction: the
security framework still quantifies over PPT adversaries.

Another application of PRGs is developed in
[[id:45474AD9-A163-4CC9-983A-78EFD52E93C9][Bit Commitments and Coin Flipping]].
