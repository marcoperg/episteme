RSA publicly raises a residue to an exponent modulo a composite integer. A
secret inverse exponent reverses that map. Its correctness is algebraic; its
one-wayness is a separate assumption, and raw RSA is not secure encryption.

* Key generation and algorithms

Fix an odd public exponent \(e\geq3\) and a prime bit length
\(\ell=\ell(\lambda)\) in an efficiently sampleable parameter regime.

1. Sample a uniform \(\ell\)-bit prime \(p\) with \(\gcd(e,p-1)=1\).
2. Sample a uniform \(\ell\)-bit prime \(q\ne p\) with \(\gcd(e,q-1)=1\).
3. Compute \(N=pq\) and the positive inverse exponent
   \(d=e^{-1}\bmod (p-1)(q-1)\).
4. Publish \(pk=(N,e)\) and retain \(sk=(N,d)\).

The gcd conditions ensure that \(d\) exists. Distinctness of the primes is
essential. The key-dependent domain and codomain are both \(\mathbb Z_N\):

\[
F((N,e),x)=x^e\bmod N,\qquad I((N,d),y)=y^d\bmod N.
\]

Both operations use efficient modular exponentiation. Computing \(d\) from
the factorization uses \(\varphi(N)=(p-1)(q-1)\) and extended Euclid
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.7].

* Correctness on every residue

The exponent relation gives \(ed=1+a(p-1)(q-1)\) for an integer \(a\geq0\).
For every integer \(x\), work modulo \(p\):

- If \(p\mid x\), both \(x^{ed}\) and \(x\) are zero.
- Otherwise Fermat gives
  \(x^{ed}=x(x^{p-1})^{a(q-1)}=x\pmod p\).

The same argument holds modulo \(q\). CRT therefore gives

\[
x^{ed}=x\pmod N
\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb Z_N.
\]

Hence \(I(sk,F(pk,x))=x\). Injectivity on the finite set \(\mathbb Z_N\)
makes the public map a permutation with the stated inverse.

Euler's theorem alone would prove only the unit case. Treating zero residues
modulo each prime separately is what covers nonunits
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.7.2].

* A small algebraic example

Take \(p=11,q=13,e=7\). Then \(N=143\), \(\varphi(N)=120\), and
\(d=103\), since \(7\cdot103=1+6\cdot120\). For example,

\[
42^7\bmod143=81,\qquad81^{103}\bmod143=42.
\]

The nonunit \(11\) also works: its image is \(132\), and inversion returns
\(11\). These tiny numbers illustrate correctness only; exhaustive inversion
is easy.

* Raw RSA does not hide chosen messages

The formula \(c=m^e\bmod N\) is deterministic. Given two candidate
messages, anyone can compute their images and compare them with the challenge.
Correctness makes the two images distinct, so this gives a perfect
distinguishing attack.

The RSA assumption instead concerns /recovering a uniformly sampled preimage/.
It does not establish semantic security for messages chosen by an adversary
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.10.2].
