A one-way trapdoor scheme establishes a random preimage resistant to passive
full recovery, not necessarily an indistinguishable or authenticated session key.

* Protocol and correctness

For a trapdoor scheme \(\mathcal T=(G,F,I)\):

1. Alice generates \((pk,sk)\leftarrow G(1^\lambda)\) and sends \(pk\).
2. Bob samples uniform \(X\in\mathcal X\), sends \(Y=F(pk,X)\), and
   outputs \(X\).
3. Alice outputs \(X'=I(sk,Y)\).

Bob uses \(\mathcal X_{pk}\) for a key-dependent domain. Trapdoor correctness
gives \(X'=X\); the public transcript is \((pk,Y)\)
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.3].

* An exact passive-recovery reduction

An inverter \(B\) gives passive recovery adversary \(A\) its challenge
\((pk,F(pk,X))\) as a transcript and returns its guess. The challenge
distributions and winning events coincide:

\[
\operatorname{KEAdv}[A,\mathcal P_{\mathcal T}]
=\operatorname{OWAdv}[B,\mathcal T].
\]

There is no guessing loss. For RSA, the transcript is \((N,e,X^e\bmod N)\)
and recovery advantage equals RSA inversion advantage
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.10].

* Why the raw output is not a pseudorandom session key

Given the transcript and a proposed key \(Z\), anyone can test \(F(pk,Z)=Y\).

It accepts the real \(Z=X\) with probability \(1\), but an independent
uniform \(Z\) with probability \(1/|\mathcal X|\), by injectivity. This
distinguishes the joint distributions with advantage \(1-1/|\mathcal X|\)
without finding \(X\)
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.3.1].

Key derivation needs its own model and proof. The
[[id:91B8E8A6-F67B-4918-AFB4-6AD0087AC6C8][one-way-function note]] gives a separate input-bit leakage example.

* Active substitution is outside the theorem

On a common fixed domain, substitute the attacker's public key for Alice's,
invert Bob's reply with the attacker's trapdoor, then re-encode \(X\) under
Alice's real key. Both parties agree, but the attacker knows their secret.

Substituting an RSA modulus likewise exposes Bob's output. Different moduli
give different domains, so relaying the /same/ raw value to Alice is not
automatic. Neither theorem supplies authentication
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.10.1].
