Ordinary RSA permutes the key-dependent space \(\mathbb Z_N\). A fixed-domain
extension applies RSA inside complete blocks of a common space and fixes the
incomplete tail. That easy tail must have negligible measure.

* Construction

For moduli made from two \(\ell\)-bit primes, choose \(s>2\ell\) and set

\[
M=2^s,\quad\mathcal X=\mathcal Y=\{0,\ldots,M-1\},
\quad a=\lfloor M/N\rfloor,\quad r=M-aN<N.
\]

For \(x=jN+u\), where \(0\leq u<N\), define

\begin{equation*}
F^*((N,e),x)=
\begin{cases}
jN+(u^e\bmod N),&0\leq j<a,\\
x,&aN\leq x<M.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}

Inversion uses exponent \(d\) within complete blocks and the identity on the
tail. Quotient/remainder arithmetic is efficient
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.8].

These disjoint blocks exhaust the domain. Permuting each complete block and
fixing the tail proves correctness and bijectivity on the /same/ \(s\)-bit
space for every key.

* Why a merely larger domain is insufficient

The tail is publicly invertible. A uniform input lands there with probability
\(r/M<N/M\).

The condition \(M>N\) alone does not make that probability negligible. Taking
\(s=2\ell+\lambda\), with \(N<2^{2\ell}\), ensures
\(N/M<2^{-\lambda}\). Representation length stays polynomial even though
the domain has exponentially many elements.

* Reduction to RSA inversion

For every inverter \(A\), there is an RSA inverter \(B\) such that

\[
\operatorname{OWAdv}[A,\mathcal T^*]
\leq\operatorname{RSAAdv}[B]+\mathbb E_N[N/M].
\]

Condition on \(N\). Let \(\alpha_N\) be success on a uniform complete-block
input. Total success is at most
\((aN/M)\alpha_N+r/M\leq\alpha_N+N/M\).

Given \((N,e,Y)\), choose uniform \(j\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}\), run \(A\)
on \(jN+Y\), and return the remainder of a valid inverse in that block. This
exactly simulates a uniform complete-block challenge, giving success
\(\alpha_N\). Averaging over keys proves the bound
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 8.9.2].

Under the RSA assumption and the stated choice of \(s\), both terms are
negligible. The identity tail preserves bijectivity; its negligible probability
is what preserves inversion hardness.
