A PRF provides secret pads indexed by public addresses. Choosing a fresh random
address for each encryption permits many messages under one fixed key without
a synchronized stream position.

* Construction and correctness

Let \(F:\mathcal K\times\{0,1\}^d\to\{0,1\}^{\ell}\) be a secure PRF.
To encrypt \(m\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\), sample uniform \(r\in\{0,1\}^d\)
independently on every call and output

\[
E(k,m)=(r,F(k,r)\oplus m).
\]

Decrypt by \(D(k,(r,c))=F(k,r)\oplus c\), rejecting malformed encodings.
XOR cancellation proves correctness. The public index \(r\) identifies a
pad; it does not reveal its value
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 5.3].

* CPA-security theorem

For every adversary \(A\) making at most \(Q\) encryption queries, there is
a PRF distinguisher \(B\) making at most \(Q\) oracle queries such that

\[
\operatorname{CPAAdv}[A,\Pi]
\leq 2\operatorname{PRFAdv}[B,F]+\frac{Q(Q-1)}{2^d}.
\]

Thus the scheme is CPA secure if \(2^d\) is super-polynomial in the security
parameter. The query bound \(Q\) is polynomial, as the adversary is PPT.

* Proof by three games

1. *Real:* answer queries with pads \(F(k,r_i)\).
2. *Random function:* replace \(F_k\) by one random function \(R\). A PRF
   distinguisher chooses its own hidden bit, simulates encryption using its
   oracle, and outputs whether \(A\) guessed that bit. The change in winning
   probability is \(\operatorname{PRFAdv}[B,F]\).
3. *Independent pads:* use a fresh uniform pad on every query, even at repeated
   indices. Couple this with game 2 until some \(r_i=r_j\). The collision
   probability is at most \(\binom Q2/2^d\). In game 3 the transcript is
   independent of the hidden bit, even for adaptive plaintext queries.

The guessing advantage is at most the PRF term plus the collision probability.
Multiplication by two gives the stated distinguishing bound.

* Limits and counterexamples

Distinct indices give independent pads only in the random-function game.
Random indices can repeat: then \(c_i\oplus c_j=m_i\oplus m_j\).
Security therefore has a usage-dependent collision term.

The XOR construction needs a bitstring-output PRF. Arbitrary encodings of
group-valued PRFs are not automatically uniform-looking bitstrings. Also,
\((r,c\oplus\Delta)\) decrypts to \(m\oplus\Delta\), so the construction
does not authenticate ciphertexts.
