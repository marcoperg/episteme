CBC combines a fresh random IV with ciphertext feedback. Its CPA theorem
requires the IV to be sampled after the current plaintext pair is fixed;
merely choosing distinct IVs is insufficient.

* Construction and correctness

Let \(P\) be a block cipher on \(\{0,1\}^n\). For a message of at most
\(L\) whole blocks, sample a fresh independent uniform \(c_0\) and compute

\[
c_j=P_k(m_j\oplus c_{j-1}),\qquad 1\leq j\leq v.
\]

Output \((c_0,c_1,\ldots,c_v)\). Recover

\[
m_j=P_k^{-1}(c_j)\oplus c_{j-1}.
\]

The inverse identity and XOR cancellation prove correctness. The IV adds one
block of overhead. Non-whole-block messages require an injective, unambiguously
removable padding rule; the following experiment uses whole blocks and
equal-block-count messages [cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 5.8].

* CPA-security bound

Set \(N=2^n\) and \(T=QL\), where \(Q\) bounds the number of encryption
queries. For every \(A\), there is a forward-oracle PRP adversary \(B\)
using at most \(T\) primitive queries such that

\[
\operatorname{CPAAdv}^{*}[A,\Pi_{\mathrm{CBC}}]
\leq\operatorname{PRPAdv}[B,P]+\frac{T(T-1)}{N}.
\]

In particular, for the distinguishing normalization,

\[
\operatorname{CPAAdv}[A,\Pi_{\mathrm{CBC}}]
\leq2\operatorname{PRPAdv}[B,P]+\frac{2Q^2L^2}{N}.
\]

Hence secure PRPs with super-polynomial block spaces yield CPA-secure CBC for
polynomial \(Q,L\) [cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 5.8.1].

* Proof: two different statistical errors

1. In the bit-guessing game, replace \(P_k\) by a random function \(R\).
   A forward distinguisher simulates the encryption recurrence and tests the
   final guess. PRP-PRF switching bounds the change by
   \(\operatorname{PRPAdv}[B,P]+\binom T2/N\).
2. Replace every IV and ciphertext block by independent uniform blocks
   \(Y_{i,0},\ldots,Y_{i,L}\). Retain virtual primitive inputs
   \(X_{i,j}=m_{i,b,j}\oplus Y_{i,j-1}\). Couple the games until two
   virtual inputs coincide. Their collision probability is at most
   \(\binom T2/N\).
3. In the independent-block game, the complete transcript depends on lengths,
   not on the challenge bit. Its guessing success is \(1/2\).

For the collision step, condition on all earlier responses and the adversary's
coins. The /whole current message/ is fixed before its fresh independent
\(Y\)-blocks are sampled. Its virtual inputs are therefore independent
uniform values, each uniform relative to any fixed earlier input. This handles
adaptive queries without assuming that messages were fixed in advance.

The random-function hybrid need not be an invertible cipher: the CPA
adversary has no decryption oracle.

* Predictable-IV attack

Suppose a prior one-block query \((m,m)\) returned \((r,c)\), and the next
IV \(r'\) is known before choosing the next messages. For nonzero
\(\Delta\), submit

\[
m_0=m\oplus r\oplus r',\qquad m_1=m_0\oplus\Delta.
\]

The next ciphertext block equals \(c\) exactly when \(m_0\) was chosen.
This perfectly distinguishes the bit, even if \(r'\ne r\). Randomness and
the timing of IV selection are security conditions
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 5.8.2].

* Limits and connections

CBC encryption is sequential; decryption can evaluate its inverse calls in
parallel. Changing only the IV to \(c_0\oplus\Delta\) changes only the
first recovered block to \(m_1\oplus\Delta\). CBC alone supplies no integrity
or decryption-oracle guarantee.

- [[id:5C5419BF-79B9-4F55-8FF5-6C31F8A5FB54][PRP-PRF switching]] accounts for the first birthday term.
- [[id:602FA248-EC73-4A4B-B17F-41A6060AAFFF][MACs]] address a different goal: preventing accepted forgeries.
