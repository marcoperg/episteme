Counter mode expands one public starting index into many secret pads. Its
security depends on avoiding reuse of /any counter input/, not merely on
choosing different starting indices.

* Construction and correctness

Let \(N=2^n\), identify counters with \(\mathbb Z_N\), and take a PRF
\(F:\mathcal K\times\mathbb Z_N\to\{0,1\}^w\). For a message
\(m=(m_1,\ldots,m_v)\) of \(w\)-bit blocks, where \(v\leq L\leq N\):

1. Sample a fresh independent uniform IV \(r\in\mathbb Z_N\).
2. Compute \(c_j=m_j\oplus F(k,r+j-1\bmod N)\), for \(1\leq j\leq v\).
3. Output \((r,c_1,\ldots,c_v)\).

Decryption applies the same XOR operations. Reject malformed encodings and
messages outside the declared length bound. Correctness is blockwise XOR
cancellation; neither party needs an inverse for \(F\). Counter wraparound is
allowed, but more than \(N\) blocks would repeat a pad within one message
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons secs. 5.4 and 5.5].

One \(n\)-bit IV serves the whole message. Pads can be computed in parallel or
in advance, and any block can be decrypted directly. A partial final block can
use the corresponding prefix of its pad; the theorem below is stated for whole
blocks and equal-block-count challenge messages.

* The right collision event

A message beginning at \(r\) uses a cyclic interval of counter inputs. For
example, messages starting at \(r\) and \(r+1\) reuse pads even though their
IVs differ. Reused pads reveal the XOR of the corresponding plaintext blocks.

For independent uniform starts \(r,r'\), two length-\(L\) cyclic intervals
intersect exactly when

\[
r'-r\in\{-(L-1),\ldots,L-1\}\pmod N.
\]

If \(2L\leq N\), there are \(2L-1\) such residues. Among \(Q\) starts,
the union bound therefore gives

\[
\Pr[\mathsf{Overlap}]
\leq\binom Q2\frac{2L-1}{N}\leq\frac{Q^2L}{N}.
\]

This also covers adaptive, variable-length messages: pre-sample all \(Q\)
starts and extend every used interval to length \(L\), including unused
positions [cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 5.5.1].

* CPA-security bound and proof

Assume \(2L\leq N\). For every \(A\) making at most \(Q\) encryption queries, there is
a PRF distinguisher \(B\), using at most \(QL\) primitive queries, such that

\[
\operatorname{CPAAdv}[A,\Pi_{\mathrm{CTR}}]
\leq 2\operatorname{PRFAdv}[B,F]
 +2\binom Q2\frac{2L-1}{N}
\leq 2\operatorname{PRFAdv}[B,F]+\frac{2Q^2L}{N}.
\]

In the bit-guessing game, first replace \(F_k\) by a random function. A PRF
distinguisher samples the hidden bit and tests whether \(A\) guesses it, so
the change in winning probability is its PRF advantage. Next replace every
block pad by an independent uniform value. Couple the games until a counter
input repeats; the overlap bound controls their divergence. Independent pads
give a transcript independent of the hidden bit. Doubling the resulting
guessing bound gives the displayed distinguishing bound.

For polynomial \(Q,L\) and super-polynomial \(N\), the statistical term is
negligible. Usage limits are part of the theorem, not an optional engineering
detail.

* Limits and connections

Changing \(c_j\) to \(c_j\oplus\Delta\) changes \(m_j\) to
\(m_j\oplus\Delta\). Counter mode does not authenticate ciphertexts.
