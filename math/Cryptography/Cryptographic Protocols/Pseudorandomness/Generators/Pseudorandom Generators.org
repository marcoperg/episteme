A pseudorandom generator expands a short uniform seed into a longer string
that looks uniform to efficient observers. It creates computationally useful
randomness, not new information-theoretic entropy.

* Definition and distinguishing experiment

Let \(L(n)>n\) be efficiently computable and polynomially bounded. A PRG is
an efficient deterministic function

\[
G:\{0,1\}^n\longrightarrow\{0,1\}^{L(n)}
\]

whose output on a uniform seed is computationally indistinguishable from a
uniform \(L(n)\)-bit string. For a PPT test \(A\), define

\[
\operatorname{PRGAdv}[A,G]
=\left|\Pr[A(G(S))=1]-\Pr[A(R)=1]\right|,
\]

where \(S\) and \(R\) are independent uniform strings of lengths \(n\) and
\(L(n)\). The parameter is also available to \(A\). Security requires
negligible advantage for every such \(A\)
[cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons sec. 2.3.1].

The same definition applies to efficiently represented and uniformly sampleable
finite spaces \(G:S\to R\), using uniform distributions on those spaces.
Expansion then means \(|R|>|S|\).

* Why the output is not actually uniform

There are at most \(2^n\) possible outputs. A test for membership in
\(\operatorname{Im}(G)\) always accepts a generated sample and accepts a
uniform sample with probability at most \(2^{n-L}\). Its advantage is at
least \(1-2^{n-L}\). Enumerating all seeds implements this test, but generally
takes exponential time.

Thus the output's Shannon entropy is at most \(n\) bits even though efficient
tests cannot detect the deficit. Statistical difference and computational
indistinguishability are compatible.

* Examples and non-examples

The map \(s\mapsto s\mathbin\Vert s\) is not a secure generator: checking
equality of the two halves distinguishes it from uniform with advantage
\(1-2^{-n}\). Correct length and balanced individual bits are not enough.

If \(f\) is a one-way permutation and \(h\) is hard-core for it, then
\(G(s)=f(s)\mathbin\Vert h(s)\) is a secure one-bit expansion. The hard-core
property replaces \(h(s)\) by an independent uniform bit; permutation
uniformity makes the resulting whole string uniform.

General OWFs also suffice for PRGs by the HILL theorem. That theorem needs
additional ideas and is not proved by merely appending a hard-core bit to an
arbitrary OWF [cite:@fioreCryptographicProtocolosApplicatons secs. 2.4 and 3.1].
