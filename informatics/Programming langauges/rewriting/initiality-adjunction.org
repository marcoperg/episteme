* Equational specification and free algebras

Fix a finitary, many-sorted equational specification
\begin{equation*}
\begin{gathered}
\Sigma=(S,\Omega),\qquad
\text{each }\omega\in\Omega\text{ has an arity }
\omega:s_1\cdots s_n\to s\quad(n<\infty),\\
E=\{\forall\vec{x}\;t=u\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation*}
Here \(S\) is the set of /sorts/, and \(\Omega\) is a set of typed
/operation symbols/. Thus \(\omega\) is a metavariable ranging over
\(\Omega\), not a single distinguished operation. Its displayed arity
says that \(\omega\) takes an
argument of each sort \(s_1,\ldots,s_n\) and returns one of sort \(s\).
The case \(n=0\) is a constant symbol. ``Finitary'' means only that every
operation has finitely many arguments.

Terms are built recursively from sorted variables and these operation
symbols. Each member \(\forall\vec{x}\;t=u\) of \(E\) equates two terms
of the same sort; the displayed variables include all variables occurring
in them. A \(\Sigma\)-algebra \(A\) interprets this syntax by assigning a
carrier set \(A_s\) to every sort and a function
\[
\omega^A:A_{s_1}\times\cdots\times A_{s_n}\to A_s
\]
to every \(\omega\in\Omega\). It is a /model/ of \(E\), written
\(A\models E\), when every equation has equal interpretations under every
sorted assignment of its variables.

Write \(\mathcal A=\mathbf{Alg}(\Sigma,E)\) for the category of these
models. A homomorphism \(h:A\to B\) is a sorted family
\(h_s:A_s\to B_s\) preserving every operation:
\[
h_s(\omega^A(a_1,\ldots,a_n))
=\omega^B(h_{s_1}(a_1),\ldots,h_{s_n}(a_n)).
\]
We allow empty carriers; with a nonempty-carrier convention, assume
instead that every sort has a ground term.

The forgetful functor retains carriers and underlying maps:
\[
U:\mathcal A\to\mathbf{Set}^{S},\qquad UA=(A_s)_{s\in S}.
\]
For a sorted set of generators \(X=(X_s)_{s\in S}\), define
\[
F(X)=T_\Sigma(X)/{\equiv_{E,X}},
\]
where \(T_\Sigma(X)\) is the term algebra and \(\equiv_{E,X}\) is the
least congruence containing all well-sorted substitution instances of
\(E\) in that algebra. Operations act on representatives:
\[
\omega^{FX}([t_1],\ldots,[t_n])=[\omega(t_1,\ldots,t_n)].
\]
For \(g:X\to Y\), the homomorphism \(Fg:FX\to FY\) replaces each
generator \(x\) by \(g(x)\). Renaming respects identities and
composition, making \(F\) a functor.

** Running example: unary naturals

Take one sort, the operations
\[
z:\to\mathsf N,\qquad s:\mathsf N\to\mathsf N,
\]
and no equations. An algebra is simply a set \(A\) with a chosen element
\(z^A\) and an endomap \(s^A:A\to A\). For one generator
\(X=\{x\}\), the free algebra consists of the distinct terms
\[
FX=\{z,s(z),s^2(z),\ldots,x,s(x),s^2(x),\ldots\}.
\]
Thus it contains two chains: one generated by the constant and one by
\(x\).

* The adjunction: assignments extend uniquely

The free--forgetful adjunction is[fn:adj]
\[
\mathbf{Set}^{S}
\underset{U}{\overset{F}{\rightleftarrows}}\mathcal A,
\qquad F\dashv U,
\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal A(FX,A)\cong\mathbf{Set}^{S}(X,UA)}.
\]
Define \(\eta_X:X\to UFX\) by \(\eta_X(x)=[x]\). The bijection sends
\[
k:FX\to A\quad\longmapsto\quad Uk\circ\eta_X:X\to UA.
\]
Conversely, an arbitrary assignment \(f:X\to UA\) extends to a unique
homomorphism \(\widehat f:FX\to A\), recursively determined by
\begin{align*}
\widehat f([x])&=f(x),\\
\widehat f([\omega(t_1,\ldots,t_n)])
  &=\omega^A(\widehat f([t_1]),\ldots,\widehat f([t_n])).
\end{align*}
Evaluation respects \(\equiv_{E,X}\) because \(A\models E\), so this
is well-defined. Structural induction gives uniqueness. Thus
\[
\boxed{U\widehat f\circ\eta_X=f}.
\]
*Freeness means that choosing the images of generators is the only choice.*

In the running example, choosing \(f(x)=a\in A\) forces
\[
\widehat f(s^n(z))=(s^A)^n(z^A),\qquad
\widehat f(s^n(x))=(s^A)^n(a).
\]
There is no further choice, and no equations must be checked.

The bijection is /natural/: for \(g:X'\to X\) and a homomorphism
\(\ell:A\to B\), renaming generators and then interpreting gives
\[
\widehat{U\ell\circ f\circ g}=\ell\circ\widehat f\circ Fg.
\]

* Unit and counit

The adjunction has natural transformations[fn:unit]
\[
\eta:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{Set}^{S}}\Rightarrow UF,
\qquad
\varepsilon:FU\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal A}.
\]
The *unit* \(\eta_X(x)=[x]\) regards a generator as a term. It need not
be injective: the equations may identify generators.

The *counit* is
\[
\varepsilon_A=\widehat{\mathrm{id}_{UA}}:F(UA)\to A.
\]
Here every element \(a\in A_s\) becomes a formal generator
\(\underline a\). The counit evaluates terms using the operations of
\(A\), with \(\varepsilon_A([\underline a])=a\). Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\widehat f=\varepsilon_A\circ Ff}.
\]
The /triangle identities/ are[fn:unit]
\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{FX}\circ F(\eta_X)=\mathrm{id}_{FX},
\qquad
U(\varepsilon_A)\circ\eta_{UA}=\mathrm{id}_{UA}.
}
\]
The first says that re-expressing a term using its generators as names
and evaluating in \(FX\) returns that term. The second says that naming
an element and evaluating returns that element. These identities ensure
that restriction and extension are mutually inverse.

** The running example with one generator

Recall the signature \(z:\to\mathsf N\), \(s:\mathsf N\to\mathsf N\)
with no equations. The unit
\[
\eta_X:\{x\}\to UFX,\qquad x\longmapsto[x]
\]
does not interpret \(x\) as an element of some algebra. It merely inserts
the generator into the syntax of the free algebra, where \([x]\),
\([s(x)]\), and so on are terms.

Now let \(A=(A,z^A,s^A)\) be any algebra for this signature. In
\(F(UA)\), every actual element \(a\in A\) is treated as a new formal
generator \(\underline a\). Its terms are
\[
F(UA)=
\{s^n(z)\mid n\geq0\}\;\amalg\;
\{s^n(\underline a)\mid a\in A,\ n\geq0\}.
\]
The counit removes this extra layer of syntax by evaluation:
\[
\varepsilon_A([s^n(z)])=(s^A)^n(z^A),\qquad
\varepsilon_A([s^n(\underline a)])=(s^A)^n(a).
\]

For an assignment \(f:X\to UA\) with \(f(x)=a\), the factorization
\(\widehat f=\varepsilon_A\circ Ff\) is visible term by term:
\begin{align*}
[s^n(x)]&\xmapsto{Ff}[s^n(\underline a)]
           \xmapsto{\varepsilon_A}(s^A)^n(a),\\
[s^n(z)]&\xmapsto{Ff}[s^n(z)]
           \xmapsto{\varepsilon_A}(s^A)^n(z^A).
\end{align*}
Thus \(Ff\) performs syntactic substitution, while \(\varepsilon_A\)
performs semantic evaluation.

The triangle identities reduce to equally concrete round trips. On a
term containing \(x\), the first one gives
\[
[s^n(x)]
\xmapsto{F(\eta_X)}[s^n(\underline{[x]})]
\xmapsto{\varepsilon_{FX}}[s^n(x)],
\]
while the second gives, for every \(a\in A\),
\[
a\xmapsto{\eta_{UA}}[\underline a]
 \xmapsto{U(\varepsilon_A)}a.
\]

* Initiality: no generators, no choices

Let \(0=(\varnothing)_{s\in S}\), the initial sorted set. Then
\[
I:=F0=T_\Sigma(0)/{\equiv_{E,0}}.
\]
For every \(A\in\mathcal A\), the adjunction gives
\[
\mathcal A(I,A)\cong\mathbf{Set}^{S}(0,UA)\cong\{\ast\}.
\]
Therefore *\(I\) is initial*: there is exactly one homomorphism
\(h_A:I\to A\). This proves, in this instance, that left adjoints
preserve initial objects.[fn:initial]

Explicitly, writing \(t^A\) for the value of a ground term,
\[
\boxed{h_A([t])=t^A},\qquad
h_A=\varepsilon_A\circ F(!),\quad !:0\to UA.
\]
There are no generator images to choose. Notice that \(h_A\) is /not/
the counit: \(\varepsilon_A\) is always surjective because every
element of \(A\) was supplied as a generator; \(h_A\) need not be.

More generally, \((FX,\eta_X)\) is initial in the /comma category/
\(X\downarrow U\): objects are assignments \(X\to UA\), and arrows
are homomorphisms preserving those assignments. At \(X=0\), the
assignment adds no data, leaving ordinary initiality.[fn:adj]

In the running example, removing \(x\) leaves only
\[
I=\{z,s(z),s^2(z),\ldots\}\cong\mathbb N,
\]
with \(z^I=0\) and \(s^I(n)=n+1\). The unique homomorphism into any
pointed endomap \((A,z^A,s^A)\) is
\[
h_A(n)=(s^A)^n(z^A).
\]

* No junk and no confusion

Fix \(A\models E\) and its canonical map \(h_A:I\to A\).

*No junk* means every element is the value of a ground term:
\[
\forall s\in S\;\forall a\in A_s\;\exists t\in T_\Sigma(0)_s:
\quad t^A=a.
\]
This is exactly sortwise surjectivity of \(h_A\).

*No confusion* means that no ground terms are identified beyond the
specified congruence:
\[
\forall s\in S\;\forall t,u\in T_\Sigma(0)_s:
\quad t^A=u^A\Longrightarrow t\equiv_{E,0}u.
\]
The reverse implication already follows from \(A\models E\).
Thus no confusion is exactly sortwise injectivity of \(h_A\).
These conditions concern ground terms, not all universal identities
that happen to hold in \(A\).

The running example makes the two conditions independent:

- For \(A=\mathbb N\), with zero and successor, \(h_A\) is the identity:
  there is neither junk nor confusion.
- For \(A=\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}\), extend successor by
  \(s^A(\infty)=\infty\). The element \(\infty\) is junk, while distinct
  ground terms still have distinct values.
- For \(A=\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), with \(z^A=[0]\) and
  \(s^A([k])=[k+1]\), every element is denoted, but \(z\) and
  \(s^m(z)\) are confused.

In this category, monomorphisms are precisely sortwise injections, and
regular epimorphisms are precisely sortwise surjections. A /regular/
epimorphism is a coequalizer of some parallel pair; a surjective
homomorphism is the quotient by its kernel congruence. Hence
\begin{align*}
\text{no junk}&\iff h_A\text{ is a regular epimorphism},\\
\text{no confusion}&\iff h_A\text{ is a monomorphism}.
\end{align*}
Since a bijective homomorphism has a homomorphic inverse,
\[
\boxed{
\text{no junk + no confusion}
\iff h_A\text{ is an isomorphism}
\iff A\text{ is initial}.
}
\]
For the last equivalence, initial objects are uniquely isomorphic;
conversely, an isomorphism with \(I\) transfers its universal property.
The unique morphism from such an \(A\) to any \(B\) is
\(h_B\circ h_A^{-1}\).

*Do not replace ``regular epi'' by ``epi''.* For example, the unital-ring
inclusion \(\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q\) is mono and epi but
not an isomorphism.[fn:epi]

* The same result as a quotient statement

Work here in \(\mathbf{Alg}(\Sigma)\), since the raw term algebra
need not satisfy \(E\). Put \(T=T_\Sigma(0)\) and consider
\[
R_E\rightrightarrows T\xrightarrow{q}I\xrightarrow{h_A}A,
\qquad e_A=h_A\circ q,
\]
where \(R_E=\equiv_{E,0}\) has its two projections and \(q\) is their
coequalizer. For a homomorphism \(f\), its /kernel pair/ is the
pullback of \(f\) along itself: concretely, the pairs \((x,y)\) with
\(f(x)=f(y)\). Therefore
\[
\ker(q)=R_E\subseteq\ker(e_A),
\]
and
\begin{equation*}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{no confusion}&\iff\ker(e_A)=\ker(q),\\
\text{no junk}&\iff e_A\text{ is sortwise surjective}.
\end{aligned}
}
\end{equation*}
The quotient makes exactly the prescribed identifications; the two
conditions say that it accounts for exactly the elements and equalities
of the proposed model.
