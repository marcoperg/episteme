* The idea and the setting

*Small-step semantics specifies the next computation step.*
Repeated transitions describe execution; individual transitions may be
justified by several inference-rule applications
[cite:@pierceProgrammingLanguageFoundations2026 chap. Smallstep, introduction].

Use the finite arithmetic syntax

\[
E=\mu F,
\qquad F(X)=\mathbb Z+\mathrm{Var}+\mathrm{Op}\times X^2,
\qquad \mathrm{Op}=\{+,-,*\}.
\]

Environments \(\rho:\mathrm{Var}\to\mathbb Z\) are total and unchanged
by evaluation. Values are syntactic numerals \(\underline n\).
The operation \(\widehat o:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\) interprets \(o\).

* A left-to-right transition relation

The judgement \(\rho\vdash e\longrightarrow e'\) means one step under
\(\rho\). Define it as the least relation closed under these rules:

\[
\frac{}{\rho\vdash x\longrightarrow\underline{\rho(x)}}
\qquad
\frac{}{
\rho\vdash\underline{n_1}\mathbin{o}\underline{n_2}
\longrightarrow\underline{\widehat o(n_1,n_2)}
}
\]

\[
\frac{\rho\vdash e_1\longrightarrow e_1'}{
\rho\vdash e_1\mathbin{o}e_2\longrightarrow e_1'\mathbin{o}e_2
}
\qquad
\frac{\rho\vdash e_2\longrightarrow e_2'}{
\rho\vdash\underline{n_1}\mathbin{o}e_2
\longrightarrow\underline{n_1}\mathbin{o}e_2'
}.
\]

There is no rule reducing a numeral. The last rule permits evaluation of
the right operand only when the left is already a numeral.

#+LATEX: \Needspace{5\baselineskip}
For example, with \(\rho(x)=4\), and numeral underlining suppressed,

\[
(x+2)*3\longrightarrow(4+2)*3\longrightarrow6*3\longrightarrow18.
\]

Write \(\longrightarrow^k\) for exactly \(k\) transitions and
\(\longrightarrow^*=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb N}\longrightarrow^k\).
In particular, \(\longrightarrow^0\) is equality.

* From an inductive relation to a coalgebra

The rules above define an *inductive relation*, just as big-step rules
do. Once defined, that relation can be packaged as a *coalgebra*.
These are two descriptions of the same transition system, not competing
choices between induction and coinduction
[cite:@jacobsIntroductionCoalgebraMathematics2012 secs. 2.2.4 and 5.5].

Let \(C=\mathrm{Env}\times E\). Define

\[
\gamma:C\to\mathcal P(C),
\qquad
\gamma(\rho,e)=\{(\rho,e')\mid\rho\vdash e\longrightarrow e'\}.
\]

This is a coalgebra for the covariant powerset functor, whose action on
maps is \(\mathcal P(h)(U)=h[U]\). It exposes possible successors.
By itself, however, it does not record a final integer: all numeral
configurations have the same empty successor set. An output observation
must be included to distinguish their results.

* A deterministic coalgebra with results

For the displayed rules, structural induction establishes determinism
and progress: every non-numeral has exactly one successor.
For termination, define

\begin{align*}
m(\underline n)&=0,\\
m(x)&=1,\\
m(e_1\mathbin{o}e_2)&=1+m(e_1)+m(e_2).
\end{align*}

Induction on a one-step derivation shows that each transition decreases
\(m\) by exactly one. Together with progress, this proves that every
expression reaches a numeral in exactly \(m(e)\) transitions.
This counts semantic transitions, not machine running time.

#+LATEX: \Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For a fixed \(\rho\), we can consequently define a total map

\begin{equation*}
\boxed{
\gamma_\rho:E\to\mathbb Z+E,
\qquad
\gamma_\rho(e)=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{inl}(n),&e=\underline n,\\
\mathrm{inr}(e'),&\rho\vdash e\longrightarrow e'.
\end{cases}
}
\end{equation*}

This is a coalgebra for \(G(X)=\mathbb Z+X\), with
\(G(h)=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb Z}+h\): *return an integer or take a step*.
The behavioural functor \(G\) is not the syntax functor \(F\).

* Whole executions through a final coalgebra

An initial algebra supplies a unique /fold/ out of syntax; a final
coalgebra supplies a unique /behaviour map/ out of a transition system.
The latter must preserve observations and successors
[cite:@jacobsIntroductionCoalgebraMathematics2012 secs. 1.2 and 2.3].
For the dual initial-algebra construction, see
[[id:2D0BB075-2A72-4BD2-B116-7ABEBE2AE647][Initial algebra of an endofunctor]].

For this particular \(G\), the final coalgebra has carrier

\[
Q=\nu G\cong(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb N)+\{\infty\},
\]

where \((n,k)\) means returning \(n\) after \(k\) transitions, and
\(\infty\) means continuing forever. Its structure map
\(\mathsf{out}:Q\to\mathbb Z+Q\) is

\begin{align*}
\mathsf{out}(n,0)&=\mathrm{inl}(n),\\
\mathsf{out}(n,k+1)&=\mathrm{inr}(n,k),\\
\mathsf{out}(\infty)&=\mathrm{inr}(\infty).
\end{align*}

Every \(G\)-coalgebra state either returns a particular integer after a
unique finite number of transitions or continues forever. Sending states
to those outcomes defines the unique coalgebra morphism into \(Q\).
Applied to our evaluator, this gives

\[
\mathsf{beh}_\rho:E\to Q,
\qquad
\boxed{
\mathsf{out}\circ\mathsf{beh}_\rho
=G(\mathsf{beh}_\rho)\circ\gamma_\rho.
}
\]

In particular,

\[
\mathsf{beh}_\rho(e)=(n,k)
\iff\rho\vdash e\longrightarrow^k\underline n.
\]

Here \(\infty\) is never reached, by the termination argument above.
This choice of functor remembers the result and step count, /not/ the
intermediate expression trees. Other observations require a different
behavioural functor; stuck states would also require a separate case.
