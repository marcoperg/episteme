* The idea

*Syntax supplies constructors; semantics supplies their interpretations.*
For an inductively defined syntax, the interpretation extends uniquely to
all terms by structural recursion. Categorically, this is a homomorphism
from an initial algebra into a chosen semantic algebra
[cite:@goguenInitialAlgebraSemantics1977].

We work in \(\mathbf{Set}\), with ordinary, non-indexed algebraic inductive
types. This suffices for the arithmetic language below.

* Minimal preliminaries

An *endofunctor* \(F:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}\) describes one layer of
syntax. Replace recursive occurrences of the type by \(X\): constructor
alternatives become coproducts (disjoint unions), simultaneous arguments
become products, and nonrecursive argument types stay fixed.
On functions, \(F\) maps the recursive positions and preserves the rest.

An *\(F\)-algebra* (see [[id:F5B69F90-6182-4E71-9585-FFE49AA48942][Algebra for an endofunctor]]) is a carrier \(A\)
equipped with a map
\(a:F(A)\to A\): a way to combine one layer of inputs into a result.
The inductive type \(T=\mu F\), with its constructors bundled into

\[
\mathsf{in}:F(T)\to T,
\]

is the *initial \(F\)-algebra* (see [[id:2D0BB075-2A72-4BD2-B116-7ABEBE2AE647][Initial algebra of an endofunctor]]):
there is exactly one algebra homomorphism
from it to every other \(F\)-algebra. This is structural recursion with
uniqueness, not merely the fixed-point equation \(T\cong F(T)\)
[cite:@goguenInitialAlgebraSemantics1977].

For the connection with free algebras and assignments of generators, see
[[file:../rewriting/initiality-adjunction.org][Initiality and the Free--Forgetful Adjunction]].

* Denotational semantics as a fold

Choose a *semantic domain* \(D\) and a *semantic algebra*
\(\alpha:F(D)\to D\). The latter interprets each constructor using the
meanings of its immediate subterms, rather than the subterms themselves.

Initiality gives the unique interpretation map, called a *fold* or
*catamorphism* [cite:@meijerFunctionalProgrammingBananas1991 secs. 3-4].[fn:folds]

\[
\boxed{
[\![-]\!]=\mathsf{fold}_{\alpha}:T\to D.
}
\]

Being an algebra homomorphism means precisely that

\[
\boxed{
[\![-]\!]\circ\mathsf{in}
=
\alpha\circ F([\![-]\!]).
}
\]

The left side constructs a term and then interprets it. The right side
first interprets its subterms and then combines their meanings.
*This equation expresses compositionality*
[cite:@goguenInitialAlgebraSemantics1977].

Thus \((D,\alpha)\) is the semantic choice; the map
\([\![-]\!]\) follows uniquely. Initiality does not choose the intended
operations for us, nor by itself prove agreement with an operational
semantics.

* Example: arithmetic expressions

** Syntax

\begin{gather*}
e ::= n \mid x \mid e+e \mid e-e \mid e*e,
\\
n\in\mathbb Z,\qquad x\in\mathrm{Var}.
\end{gather*}

The signature functor is

\[
F(X)=\mathbb Z+\mathrm{Var}
     +(X\times X)+(X\times X)+(X\times X).
\]

For \(h:X\to Y\), its action on functions is

\[
F(h)=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb Z}+\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Var}}
     +(h\times h)+(h\times h)+(h\times h).
\]

Here \(+\) denotes coproduct, not arithmetic addition. The five summands
are tagged \(\mathsf{num},\mathsf{var},\mathsf{add},\mathsf{sub},\mathsf{mul}\).
The finite expression trees form \(E=\mu F\); \(\mathsf{in}\) turns a tagged
layer of subexpressions into an expression.

** Semantic domain and algebra

A variable environment assigns an integer to each variable. Take

\[
\mathrm{Env}=\mathrm{Var}\to\mathbb Z,
\qquad
D=\mathrm{Env}\to\mathbb Z.
\]

An expression denotes a *function of its environment*, not a single
integer. Define \(\alpha:F(D)\to D\) by

\begin{align*}
\alpha(\mathsf{num}(n))     &= \lambda\rho.\,n,\\
\alpha(\mathsf{var}(x))     &= \lambda\rho.\,\rho(x),\\
\alpha(\mathsf{add}(f,g))   &= \lambda\rho.\,f(\rho)+g(\rho),\\
\alpha(\mathsf{sub}(f,g))   &= \lambda\rho.\,f(\rho)-g(\rho),\\
\alpha(\mathsf{mul}(f,g))   &= \lambda\rho.\,f(\rho)\cdot g(\rho).
\end{align*}

The tags here are coproduct injections; \(f,g\in D\) are already meanings.
Integer arithmetic occurs only on the right-hand sides.

** Induced interpretation

The unique homomorphism
\([\![-]\!]=\mathsf{fold}_{\alpha}:E\to D\) satisfies

\begin{align*}
[\![n]\!]\rho &= n,\\
[\![x]\!]\rho &= \rho(x),\\
[\![e_1\mathbin{\diamond}e_2]\!]\rho
 &= ([\![e_1]\!]\rho)\mathbin{\diamond}([\![e_2]\!]\rho),
\qquad \diamond\in\{+,-,*\}.
\end{align*}

On the left, \(\diamond\) is a syntax constructor; on the right, its
integer interpretation. These familiar semantic clauses are exactly the
homomorphism equation expanded constructor by constructor.
Fixing \(\rho\) gives the evaluator \(e\mapsto[\![e]\!]\rho\).

* Semantic equality

Different syntax can have the same denotation. Define

\begin{align*}
e\equiv_{\mathrm{sem}}e'
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[\![e]\!]=[\![e']\!]\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall\rho\in\mathrm{Env},\;
[\![e]\!]\rho=[\![e']\!]\rho.
\end{align*}

For example, \(x+0\equiv_{\mathrm{sem}}x\), although the syntax trees differ.
The homomorphism equation makes this equivalence a *congruence*: replacing
a subexpression by one with the same denotation preserves the meaning of
the whole expression.
