This note relates the three constructions. The example throughout uses
finite arithmetic expressions, total integer environments, and the usual
integer operations; small-step evaluation is left-to-right.

* Shared syntax, different semantic choices

We work in \(\mathbf{Set}\), with

\begin{gather*}
F(X)=\mathbb Z+\mathrm{Var}+\{+,-,*\}\times X^2,
\\
E=\mu F,
\qquad \mathrm{Env}=\mathrm{Var}\to\mathbb Z.
\end{gather*}

The map \(\mathsf{in}:F(E)\to E\) supplies the syntax constructors.
It does not choose how programs should execute.

*Denotational semantics* chooses an algebra \(\alpha:F(D)\to D\),
where \(D=\mathrm{Env}\to\mathbb Z\), and obtains

\[
[\![-]\!]=\mathsf{fold}_\alpha:E\to D,
\qquad
[\![-]\!]\circ\mathsf{in}=\alpha\circ F([\![-]\!]).
\]

*Big-step semantics* independently gives rules defining the least
relation \(\rho\vdash e\Downarrow n\): complete successful evaluations.

*Small-step semantics* independently gives rules defining
\(\rho\vdash e\longrightarrow e'\): individual transitions. With the
specified strategy and total environments, these can be packaged as
\(\gamma_\rho:E\to G(E)\), where \(G(X)=\mathbb Z+X\).

Thus \(F\) describes /syntax constructors/, while \(G\) describes
/behavioural observations/. There is no requirement that \(F=G\).
The rule-based and coalgebraic descriptions are compatible: the rules
can inductively define the very relation represented by a coalgebra
[cite:@jacobsIntroductionCoalgebraMathematics2012 sec. 5.5].

* Agreement is a theorem

For the particular semantics in these notes,

\begin{equation*}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\rho\vdash e\Downarrow n
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho\vdash e\longrightarrow^*\underline n
\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[\![e]\!]\rho=n.
\end{aligned}
}
\end{equation*}

Here \(\underline n\) is a syntactic numeral and
\(\longrightarrow^*\) allows any finite number of steps, including zero.
Agreement does not follow merely from sharing a syntax functor.

** Big-step and denotational semantics

Prove the first-to-last correspondence by structural induction on \(e\),
using inversion of the big-step rules. Constants and variables agree
immediately. For a binary constructor, both definitions combine exactly
the values of the two subexpressions using the same integer operation.
The induction hypotheses identify those values
[cite:@pierceLogicalFoundations2026 chap. Imp, sec. Equivalence of the Definitions].

** Big-step and small-step semantics

For big-step to small-step, induct on the evaluation derivation.
In the binary case, concatenate the left operand's reduction, the right
operand's reduction, and the final arithmetic step, using the context
rules to lift the subexpression reductions.

For the converse, first prove by induction on a one-step derivation that

\[
\rho\vdash e\longrightarrow e'
\;\land\;
\rho\vdash e'\Downarrow n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\rho\vdash e\Downarrow n.
\]

Starting with \(\rho\vdash\underline n\Downarrow n\), apply this lemma
backwards along a finite reduction to obtain \(\rho\vdash e\Downarrow n\).
These implications relate finite results; they do not require a separate
assumption that /every/ program terminates
[cite:@pierceProgrammingLanguageFoundations2026 chap. Smallstep, sec. Equivalence of Big-Step and Small-Step].

* Relating behaviour to denotation

The [[id:d4d9c729-f044-4b87-8a22-2e56af8b6343][small-step note]] constructs the unique behaviour map

\[
\mathsf{beh}_\rho:E\to Q,
\qquad Q=(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb N)+\{\infty\}.
\]

It records the result and number of transitions, or divergence.
Let \(\mathbb Z_\bot=\mathbb Z+\{\bot\}\), and define an abstraction

\[
q:Q\to\mathbb Z_\bot,
\qquad q(n,k)=n,
\qquad q(\infty)=\bot.
\]

Writing \(\iota:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z_\bot\) for the inclusion, agreement
and termination give

\[
\boxed{
\iota([\![e]\!]\rho)=q(\mathsf{beh}_\rho(e)).
}
\]

The denotation here forgets the transition count. For example,
\([\![x+0]\!]=[\![x]\!]\), but \(x+0\) takes two transitions and
\(x\) takes one. Equal final values therefore need not mean equal
coalgebraic behaviours for this choice of \(G\).

* Scope and generalisation

These are not exclusive mathematical categories. The big-step relation
for this language is itself recoverable by a syntax fold; small-step
rules are inductive even though executions can be studied coalgebraically.
Nor must denotational semantics forget time: a richer semantic domain
can record cost, traces, state changes or other chosen observations.

Finite syntax does not in general imply terminating execution: adding a
loop constructor still gives finite syntax trees. Ordinary inductive
big-step rules record finite successful evaluations; without additional
judgements, absence of a result does not distinguish divergence from
getting stuck. Small-step semantics exposes that distinction as an
infinite run versus a non-value with no successor
[cite:@pierceProgrammingLanguageFoundations2026 chap. Smallstep, introduction].

#+LATEX: \Needspace{8\baselineskip}
A further categorical step is /bialgebraic operational semantics/:
combine syntax and behaviour on one carrier,

\[
F(E)\xrightarrow{\mathsf{in}}E\xrightarrow{\gamma_\rho}G(E),
\]

and express their compatibility using suitable distributive-law data.
Such compatibility can ensure that behavioural equivalence is a
congruence for syntax constructors; merely giving the two maps does not
supply that guarantee
[cite:@jacobsIntroductionCoalgebraMathematics2012 sec. 5.5].
