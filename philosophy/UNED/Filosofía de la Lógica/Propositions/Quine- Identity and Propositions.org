#+title: Quine: Identity and Propositions
#+setupfile: ../../../../setup.org

* Identity and ontology

Quine's principle:

$$
\text{No entity without identity.}
$$

To admit a kind of entity into one's ontology, there must be a clear criterion for when two such entities are identical.

For sets this is straightforward:

$$
A=B \iff \forall x\,(x\in A \leftrightarrow x\in B).
$$

Thus sets possess a precise extensional identity criterion.

* The problem for propositions

If propositions are first-class entities, variables may range over them:

$$
\exists P\,\varphi(P).
$$

Quine then asks: when are two propositions \(P,Q\) identical?

Possible criteria fail:

* Same truth value:

  $$
  P=Q \iff (P\leftrightarrow Q)
  $$

  collapses all true propositions and all false propositions.

* Necessary equivalence:

  $$
  P=Q \iff \Box(P\leftrightarrow Q)
  $$

  identifies all necessarily equivalent propositions, although a subject may believe one without believing the other.

* Same linguistic expression:
  gives clear identity, but then the objects are sentences/formulas rather than language-independent propositions.

Hence propositions lack, for Quine, a satisfactory principle of individuation.

* Quine's criticism

Objectual quantification carries ontological commitment:

$$
\exists P\,\varphi(P)
$$

means that there are entities which can be values of \(P\).

Therefore, if \(P\) ranges over propositions, the theory is committed to propositions as objects. But if propositions lack determinate identity conditions, this ontology is suspect.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
\text{quantification over propositions}
\Rightarrow
\text{ontological commitment to propositions}
\Rightarrow
\text{need for identity criteria}
}
$$

and Quine's objection is that the final requirement is not satisfactorily met.

* Consequence

Quine therefore prefers, where possible, to speak about identifiable syntactic objects such as sentences or formulas rather than postulate propositions as independent first-class entities:

$$
\text{proposition } P
\quad\leadsto\quad
\ulcorner \varphi\urcorner
$$

where \(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner\) is a reified linguistic expression with clear identity conditions.
